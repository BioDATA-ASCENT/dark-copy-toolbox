Dark Copy Toolbox

Some medically important genes have near-identical twins elsewhere in the genome, so standard pipelines can't tell how many copies a person has. This toolbox walks through the problem in six steps, from why these genes are hard to which method counts them correctly, using 650 people from 1000 Genomes and copy numbers read directly off their HPRC genome assemblies.

Step 1

The problem: why some genes are “dark”

Twin genes

SMN1 and SMN2 differ at only a handful of letters; so do HBA1 and HBA2. A 150-letter sequencing read from one twin usually matches the other one perfectly.

Counting by read depth

Copy number is normally measured by counting reads: twice the reads means twice the copies. But the aligner can't tell which twin a read came from, so it flags the read as ambiguous (MAPQ 0), and most pipelines throw those reads away.

Getting the true answer

For 223 of the 650 people, HPRC has fully assembled both copies of each chromosome. We counted gene copies directly in those sequences. That's the answer key every method is scored against.

How do we know the true copy number?
  1. Use people with a finished genome. 223 of the 650 people also have an HPRC release-2 assembly: both chromosome copies (haplotypes) sequenced with long reads and assembled end to end without a reference.
  2. Find the region in each haplotype using the HPRC gene annotation, and only accept it if one continuous piece of the assembly holds every copy plus a unique gene on each side (e.g. MARVELD2 … BDP1 around SMN). A fragmented region counts as missing data, never as “0 copies”.
  3. Count the copies by sequence. Align the gene (GRCh38 SMN1 exons 1–8, or HBA2) to that stretch; every full-length match is one copy.
  4. Say which twin each copy is. SMN1 vs SMN2 is decided by the letter at c.840 in exon 7 (C = SMN1, T = SMN2), checked against all ~16 twin-specific positions; a copy missing exons 7–8 is SMN2Δ7-8. HBA1 vs HBA2 is decided by the HBA twin-specific positions.
  5. Add the two haplotypes to get the person's copy number, e.g. haplotype 1 has SMN1+SMN2, haplotype 2 has SMN1 → SMN1 = 2, SMN2 = 1.

Check: this truth agrees with DRAGEN's independent read-based calls in 201/206 people for SMN and 220/221 for α-globin. Where they disagree, the assembly can be the one that's wrong. HG00253's assembly contains the whole α-globin region twice, but its reads show only normal depth, so it is most likely an assembly error (see step 3). 206 people are usable for SMN and 221 for α-globin.

The three regions in this toolbox

Key message These genes are clinically important (SMA carrier screening, α-thalassemia), but the regions they sit in are invisible to standard pipelines. The steps below show why, and which methods can still count them.
Step 2

Look at the reads on a linear reference

What you're looking at

Read depth for the selected person along the region, on the old reference GRCh38 (top) and the complete T2T-CHM13 (bottom). Depth is converted to copy number, so 2 = the normal two copies.

How to read it

  • Blue: all reads.
  • Orange: only reads the aligner places confidently (MAPQ ≥ 20).
  • Grey band: the range where 80% of the 650 people fall.
  • Yellow ticks: the few positions where the twins differ.
  • Where blue stays near 2 and orange drops to 0, the reads are present but their placement is ambiguous. That's the “dark” part. Hover for exact values.

Key message Over SMN1/SMN2, confident reads disappear on both references, because the twins are real. On chr21, GRCh38 contains a fake extra copy of CBS/U2AF1/CRYAA, so reads split between the two and CBS looks lost. CHM13 removes the fake copy and the genes come back at 2.
Step 3

See the region as a pangenome

What you're looking at

Instead of one reference, here are many people's own assembled sequences of the region (from unique gene to unique gene), plus GRCh38 and CHM13. They are merged into a local pangenome graph with pggb, built for this toolbox. No reads were aligned to it.

How to read it

  • Rows: one haplotype (one chromosome copy). Coloured boxes are genes; blue and orange are the two twins, typed from the sequence. Hover a box for details, or click a row to select that person.
  • 1D picture: grey = this part of the graph is passed once; warm colours = passed several times (the extra copies).
  • 2D picture: unique sequence is a straight line; duplicated sequence folds into loops.

Key message The SMN region is 0.8–3 Mb and built differently in almost every person. That's why a 28 kb gene needs a Mb-scale view, and why Locityper can't handle it. Yet 36 Mb of sequence folds into a 1 Mb graph, because identical copies share nodes. A pangenome shows the variety, but counting copies still needs copy-specific signals (k-mers or twin-specific sites).
Step 4

One person, every answer ·

What you're looking at

For the selected person: the copy numbers in their own assembly (truth), the answers of the paralog-aware callers (DRAGEN ×3, ctyper ×2), and our open Bayesian caller's full uncertainty.

How to read it

  • Numbers are whole-genome counts (both chromosomes): SMN1 = 2 is typical, 1 = SMA carrier.
  • Heatmap: how likely each SMN1 × SMN2 combination is. Darker means more likely, and the orange ring marks the truth.
  • Use the shortcuts to jump to people where methods tend to disagree.
Key message For most people every method agrees. Differences show up in the unusual cases (carriers, 4 copies, α-globin deletions), and sometimes the assembly itself is wrong (HG00253).
Step 5

Call copy numbers yourself

What you're looking at

Our open Bayesian caller (v0) running live in your browser on all 650 people. It combines two clues: total depth says how many SMN1+SMN2 copies there are together, and the fraction of SMN1-like reads at the twin-specific positions says how they split.

The knobs

  • site error ε: how often a read shows the other twin's letter by mistake.
  • independent sites: how many of the ~16 twin-specific positions count as separate evidence (they're close together, so far fewer than 16).
  • allele-fraction concentration: how tightly the read fraction follows the true copy ratio.
  • depth weight: how much to trust depth. Prior: genotype frequencies in the other people (from DRAGEN).

SMN1: called vs truth

Rows = truth, columns = call. Green cells are correct, red cells are errors.

Is the caller's confidence honest?

Each dot groups people by the caller's stated confidence (x) and plots how often it was actually right (y). On the dashed line, confidence is honest.

Key message A simple, transparent model gets about 94–95% of people right from depth plus a few twin-specific positions. Switch the prior off to see what the reads alone can do: α-globin drops the most.
Step 6

Compare all methods against the truth

What the percentage means

The share of people for whom the method's copy number exactly equals the assembly truth (whole-genome count, both chromosomes; off by one counts as wrong). Only people with a complete assembly at that region are counted.

How to read it

  • Whiskers: 95% confidence interval. With ~200 people, overlapping whiskers mean “not clearly different”.
  • Dashed line: the score from always guessing the most common value. A method below it is worse than guessing.
  • Atypical people only: drops everyone with the common value (SMN1 = 2, α-globin = 4). This is where the clinically interesting cases are, and guessing scores 0%.

Not run: Locityper. Excluded by design. It aligns reads to candidate haplotypes, and this project does no read mapping. Its public database also has no SMN or HBA locus, and SMN would need a 1–3 Mb locus (see step 3).
Key message Paralog-aware callers that use twin-specific signals (DRAGEN; ctyper's k-mers; our Bayes v0) count SMN correctly in 91–99.5% of people. Counting confident reads alone (MAPQ ≥ 20) gets almost nobody right, and counting all reads is no better than guessing.
Info

Before you quote a number

    Glossary

    Copy number
    How many copies of a gene a person carries across both chromosomes. Most people have 2 per gene.
    Paralog / twin gene
    A near-identical copy of a gene elsewhere in the genome, made by an old duplication.
    Read depth
    How many sequencing reads cover a position. It scales with copy number.
    MAPQ
    The aligner's confidence that a read is placed correctly. 0 means it fits equally well in two or more places.
    PSV
    Paralog-specific variant: a position where the twins differ, and the only place reads can be told apart.
    Haplotype
    One of a person's two chromosome copies.
    Assembly
    A person's genome sequence reconstructed end to end from long reads, without relying on a reference.
    Pangenome graph
    Many people's sequences merged into one graph. Shared sequence becomes shared nodes and differences become branches.
    GRCh38 / CHM13
    The standard human reference (with gaps and some errors) and the first complete, gap-free one.
