Dark Copy Toolbox · method note

How ctyper counts SMN1 and SMN2

ctyper never asks where a read aligns. It compares the k-mers in a person's reads with the k-mers of every SMN copy seen in real genome assemblies, then finds the mix of copies that explains the counts. The numbers below are a toy example of 4 alleles and 8 k-mers; the real SMN panel has about 340 alleles and tens of thousands of k-mers.

1 · Collect every copy

The ctyper authors cut every copy of each gene family out of long-read assemblies (HPRC, HGSVC, CPC). Each copy is one “pangenome allele”: SMN1, SMN2, SMN2Δ7–8 and their variants.

2 · Tabulate their k-mers

For the family, keep the 31-letter words (k-mers) that occur nowhere else in the genome, and record which allele has which. Words covering a twin-specific letter separate SMN1 from SMN2.

3 · Count them in the reads

Scan the person's reads and count each word. Dividing by the depth of a single copy turns counts into “copies seen”.

4 · Solve for copies

Find the non-negative whole-number mix of alleles whose words best reproduce the counts (a regression, then rounding), and read off the gene names.

How we ran it: ctyper 1.0.5 on each person's region slices (the reads already aligned to GRCh38 or CHM13, read only for their sequences), with the matrix cut down to four gene groups (SMN_group1, HBA_group1, CBS_group1, U2AF1_group3). Reads aligned outside the slices, for example ~5% of SMN reads on a GRCh38 alt contig, are missed. The toy numbers above are illustrative; the real allele and k-mer counts come from the ctyper database.
